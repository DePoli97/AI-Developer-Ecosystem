# Monetization

This document captures how the project could become economically sustainable, ranked roughly from lowest friction to highest commitment. Nothing here is committed to yet; this is a working plan.

## Principles

Trust before revenue. Readers come for useful content, not for ads. Any monetization that erodes the editorial integrity of the project is off the table. Anything that ships must be something the author would link to anyway.

Reversible first. We try the lightest revenue mechanisms first and only invest in heavier ones once there is signal (traffic, signups, repeat readers).

Aligned incentives. We avoid affiliate categories where the highest-paying option is not the best option for the reader.

## Revenue streams, by friction

### Tier 1 - Passive, content-embedded

Affiliate links to developer tools the author genuinely uses (hosting, vector databases, observability, IDE plugins, books). Disclosed at the top of each article that contains them. Best for evergreen tutorials and "X vs Y" comparison pages.

GitHub Sponsors / Ko-fi / Buy Me a Coffee. Low-friction donation channels for readers who want to support the project. Modest revenue, but useful as a signal.

### Tier 2 - Newsletter-driven

A monthly newsletter (see `ROADMAP.md` Phase 4) is the highest-leverage asset. Once subscribers cross roughly 1,000, options include sponsored issues, classifieds-style job listings for AI/dev roles, and direct promotion of the project's own digital products.

### Tier 3 - Digital products

Paid PDFs, video courses, or notebook collections that go deeper than the free articles. The free content acts as the funnel. Examples worth exploring: a practical guide to building Claude agents, a curated prompt-engineering reference, an evals cookbook with runnable code.

### Tier 4 - Templates and starter kits

Polished, opinionated starter kits sold at a low price point ($19-$49). Lower revenue per unit than a course, but a great fit for developers who want a working baseline. Examples: a production-ready RAG starter, a Claude-tool-use scaffold, a browser-automation skeleton.

### Tier 5 - Micro-SaaS

A small hosted product spun out of one of the open-source tools in this repo. Free tier funnels to a paid tier. The open-source core stays free; the SaaS sells convenience (managed infra, dashboards, team features). High upside, much higher operational cost; only worth it after a tool has clear product-market fit.

### Tier 6 - Consulting / services

The author's time. Lowest scalability, highest hourly rate. The repository is the portfolio; consulting is the byproduct of being known for good work in a specific niche.

## What we are not doing

No display ads. They degrade reading experience and pay poorly for technical audiences.

No paid posts disguised as editorial. If something is sponsored, it is labeled. If something is an opinion, no money changed hands.

No scammy affiliate categories. No "make money with AI" funnels, no MLM-adjacent products, no shady tools.

## Tracking

Each monetization experiment should have a clearly defined hypothesis, a small budget of time, and a kill switch. Wins get documented; losses get documented too, so the project does not repeat them.
